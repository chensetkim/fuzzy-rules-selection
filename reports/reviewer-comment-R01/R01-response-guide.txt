1. WHAT R01 ASKS

Reviewer title:
“Offline selection and independent evaluation are asserted but not
reproducibly specified.”

The reviewer classified this as a MAJOR evidence/specification request
with UNCERTAIN status. The reviewer acknowledged that the manuscript
describes run-disjoint candidate generation, development validation and
final evaluation, and that Table 9 specifies the deployed 33-rule base.

However, the actual records are missing or not identified:

- Development corpus and its allocation to candidate generation,
  development validation and final evaluation.
- Run/seed membership and shared movement or attack-trace groups.
- Sample counts and attack/benign balance in each development partition.
- Support threshold s_min and confidence margin m.
- Classification threshold tau_Q.
- NSGA-II population size and generation count.
- Crossover and mutation settings and optimiser random seed.
- Candidate-pool/pruning results, rule-count bounds and selection output.
- Evidence connecting the selected output to the deployed 33 rules.
- Retained provenance supporting the claimed pre-evaluation freeze.

Why this matters:
The classifier may be reconstructible from the listed rules, while the
claimed derivation and independence of the final evaluation remain
unverifiable. Missing records are not proof of leakage, false results or
incorrect optimisation.

Closure requires concrete evidence permitting reconstruction and an
inspection of development/evaluation separation. Removing instructional
wording or adding a limitation alone does not supply that evidence.


2. ADDRESS R01 IN THIS ORDER

STEP 1 — IDENTIFY THE ACTUAL SELECTION SCRIPT AND SAVED OUTPUT

Start with the retained executable artifacts, not a narrative report.
Likely entry points are fuzzy-rules-selection/run_cars.sh and the program
it invokes. Inspect run_thfis.sh only to determine its role and whether
it belongs to the deployed flat 33-rule pipeline or a different model.
Do not assume that a script filename identifies the evaluated method.

Record:
- Script/source version or archive identity.
- Actual invocation, working directory and configuration/CLI overrides.
- Input files and output directory used for the retained selection run.
- Saved logs, parameter files, Pareto output and selected rule file.
- Relevant software versions and random-seed settings.

Expected outcome:
An identified script/configuration/output chain for the selection run
claimed in the manuscript. Distinguish executable defaults from the
settings actually used. A launcher alone is not execution provenance.

STEP 2 — RECONSTRUCT THE DEVELOPMENT CORPUS AND PARTITIONS

For candidate generation, development validation and final evaluation,
list the original run identities, seeds, configuration identities and
trace files. Include topology, mobility, attack and density information
where relevant to distinguish the runs and their shared realizations.

Record:
- Partition purpose and membership.
- Total sample count and attack/benign counts in development partitions.
- Labelling rules, including attack activation and benign observations.
- Whether and how complete runs were kept together.
- Shared movement/attack traces or other grouped dependencies.
- Data-generation conditions, including monitor-only/mitigation status.

Expected outcome:
An allocation manifest and a count summary that agree with the actual
input files. Distinguish simulation seeds from the optimiser seed.

STEP 3 — CHECK FINAL-EVALUATION SEPARATION AND FREEZE PROVENANCE

Check intersections between the partitions using run identities and
trace/group identities. Different numeric seed labels alone do not prove
independence if a common realization was reused.

Check what retained records establish when the rules, membership
functions, thresholds and deployment-selection policy were fixed
relative to final evaluation. Do not infer that rules were fixed in
advance merely because they are fixed now.

Expected outcome:
A documented separation check and an honest description of the retained
freeze history. Newly calculated hashes identify the files currently
available; they do not independently establish their historical dates.

If an untouched final evaluation cannot be established, identify the
affected evidence as development/exploratory. Discuss a fresh held-out
evaluation only for the claims that require it. Do not invent missing
history or automatically rerun every experiment.

STEP 4 — COMPARE SELECTED RULES WITH THE DEPLOYED CONFIGURATION

Compare the saved selected output with:
- Table 9 in the original working manuscript.
- The evaluated firmware's flat inference rule implementation.
- DM-2H-FuzzRID-Contiki-OS-3.0/Report/fuzzrid_flat_rules-IF THEN.txt
  as a supporting readable representation, not independent proof of
  which rules were executed.

Check all 33 antecedents and consequents, including conjunctions,
disjunctions, not-High conditions and unconstrained features. Check the
membership functions, integer rounding and aggregation settings used
offline against the deployed inference behavior. Similar rule counts
or reordered textual descriptions alone do not establish equivalence.

Expected outcome:
A rule/configuration comparison with any differences recorded. Use R02's
build/run record to connect the verified implementation to the numerical
results; a newly tested build does not retroactively identify old runs.

STEP 5 — REVISE THE MANUSCRIPT AND RESPONSE AFTER CHECKING EVIDENCE

Replace general statements with actual settings and references to the
supplied artifacts. Update Section 4.2.2, Algorithm 3 and the experimental
setup where necessary. Preserve Table 9 if its contents are verified;
correct it only through the normal suggestion/approval workflow.

If retained records permit it, provide a reproduction script or bounded
replay of selection. A new replay must be labelled as a new verification
and not substituted silently for the historical selection record.

Expected outcome:
A factual manuscript description, supplied evidence and a point-by-point
response consistent with the checks actually completed.


3. SUGGESTED EVIDENCE PACKAGE

Suggested filenames are organisational aids, not existing artifacts or
mandatory submission filenames:

- selection_manifest.csv
  Partition membership, run/seed/configuration IDs and trace identities.
- development_counts.csv
  Total, attack and benign sample counts by partition.
- optimizer_config.json or equivalent retained configuration
  Actual candidate/pruning/classification/search settings and seeds.
- selected_rulebase.txt or equivalent original selection output
  Selected antecedents/consequents and file identity.
- split_separation_check.txt
  Run/trace-group overlap checks, findings and limitations.
- freeze_provenance.txt
  Retained evidence about the timing of configuration selection.
- rule_correspondence.csv
  Selected-output, Table 9 and firmware comparison.
- reproduction_instructions.txt
  Exact invocation, inputs, software versions and expected outputs.

Share build/run identifiers with R02 rather than creating conflicting
parallel manifests. Retain the distinction between historical records,
new verification output and information that remains unavailable.


4. EXPECTED RESPONSE STRUCTURE

Write the response in this order:

1. Acknowledge the specific reproducibility omission.
2. Identify the actual data partitions and selection settings supplied.
3. State the separation checks performed and their verified results.
4. State the verified correspondence of selected and deployed rules.
5. Identify revised manuscript sections/tables and evidence filenames.
6. State any remaining limitation and leave unresolved elements open.

Use past tense for work actually completed. Do not write “verified,”
“implemented,” “provided” or “confirmed” for an intended future action.
Keep the response clear, concise, direct and tied to specific evidence.

6. RESPONSE WHEN SOME EVIDENCE REMAINS UNAVAILABLE

Use a specific statement of the missing evidence, rather than guessing
settings or treating a citation as an execution record. For example:

We agree that the retained manuscript description did not provide a
complete selection record. We recovered [available records], which
established [verified facts]. However, [specific allocation, setting or
historical record] could not be recovered, so [precise derivation or
evaluation-independence claim] remains unverified. We revised [locations]
to limit the claim to [supported scope]. [Any additional evaluation or
record-recovery action] is [actual status]. We have not treated this
disclosure as closing the remaining evidence request.

Whether a narrower integrated engineering contribution is sufficient
requires reassessment. A limitation paragraph is not equivalent to
reproducing the selection or demonstrating held-out independence.
